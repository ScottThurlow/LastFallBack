Campaign description

The Last Fall Back Act is a Washington State citizens' initiative effort to permanently end the biannual clock change. It would adopt permanent Pacific Standard Time using authority Washington already has under federal law (no act of Congress required) and cancel future spring-forward transitions. If Congress later authorizes states to observe daylight saving time year-round, the initiative includes a built-in bridge to permanent Pacific Daylight Time. The effort is grounded in peer-reviewed research on the health and safety harms of clock changes, including elevated cardiac-event and traffic-fatality risk in the days after "spring forward." Washington's 2019 Legislature already voted to make this switch, if Congress acted first. The Last Fall Back Act asks the Legislature to act now, on the state's own authority, with a ballot initiative as the fallback path.

Fact sheet

What it does
Ends seasonal clock changes; adopts permanent Pacific Standard Time now, with an automatic transition to permanent Pacific Daylight Time if Congress later authorizes it.
Legal authority
15 U.S.C. § 260a, the same federal provision Arizona and Hawaii use to stay on standard time year-round.
Fastest path
Passage by the Washington Legislature in the 2027 session; no ballot measure required if lawmakers act.
Fallback path
An Initiative to the Legislature, filed in March 2027, going to the 2028 Legislature and to the November 2028 ballot if lawmakers decline to act.
Signature requirement
About 386,000 valid signatures, a 20 to 25 percent cushion above the 308,911 statutory minimum for initiatives filed 2025–2028, per the WA Secretary of State.
Washington precedent
SHB 1196, Chapter 297, Laws of 2019: the Legislature already voted for permanent daylight time, contingent on federal authorization that still hasn't come.

Why state action, not just federal

Congress controls permanent daylight time. Washington controls whether it keeps changing its clocks. Federal law already lets a state remain on standard time year-round without congressional action. That's how Arizona and Hawaii do it today. Permanent daylight time is different: it requires Congress to authorize it, and the U.S. House passed the Sunshine Protection Act by a 308–117 vote on July 14, 2026, but the Senate has not scheduled final action. The Last Fall Back Act uses the path that's available to Washington right now rather than waiting on a federal timeline the state doesn't control.

Read the full initiative text for the exact legal language, including the transition mechanism if federal law changes.

Campaign timeline

Now through 2027 session: Constituents contact their legislators asking them to introduce and pass the Last Fall Back Act.

2027 legislative session (Jan–Apr): The fastest path: if the Legislature passes the Act, no ballot initiative is needed.

March 2027: If the session stalls, an Initiative to the Legislature is filed with the Secretary of State and signature gathering begins.

March – December 2027: Signature collection toward a target of about 386,000, with signatures due roughly ten days before the 2028 legislative session opens.

2028 legislative session: The certified initiative goes to lawmakers first. They can adopt it outright, or decline to act and send it to the ballot.

November 2028: If the 2028 Legislature declines to act, the initiative appears on the general election ballot for Washington voters to decide.

Frequently asked questions

Why permanent standard time instead of permanent daylight saving time?

States have clear federal authority to adopt permanent standard time under 15 U.S.C. § 260a, the same authority Arizona and Hawaii use today. Permanent daylight saving time requires congressional authorization that hasn't been enacted. The Last Fall Back Act takes the path available now, ending the clock change immediately, and switches to permanent daylight time automatically once Congress allows it.

How many signatures are needed for the initiative?

308,911 or more valid signatures from registered Washington voters: the statutory minimum for initiatives filed from 2025 through 2028, per the Washington Secretary of State. Because signatures are commonly rejected on review, the Secretary of State recommends a 20 to 25 percent cushion, putting the campaign's working target around 386,000.

When would the last clock change happen?

If enacted, Washington would fall back in November 2028 and the spring-forward scheduled for March 2029 would be canceled. Washington would be on permanent Pacific Standard Time.

Can I sign the petition online?

No. The website records interest and helps organize supporters. A valid initiative signature must be collected on the official paper petition once the initiative is filed with the Secretary of State.

Is this a registered political committee?

Not yet. See the privacy policy for how the campaign currently handles supporter information.
