On November 1, British Columbia won't be changing its clocks.

B.C. announced in March that its spring forward would be the province's final clock change. Yukon stopped in 2020. Starting this November, Vancouver is an hour ahead of Seattle every winter, and stays that way until March. Washington's legislature voted to end the clock change in 2019 and has been waiting on Congress ever since.

The Initiative

Three provisions. Clean logic.

The Last Fall Back Act is structured to work immediately, and to handle any future federal action gracefully.

Provision 01

Permanent Standard Time: Immediately

Once enacted, Washington observes Pacific Standard Time year-round. If the Act takes effect after a fall-back date, as it would following a November election, the very next spring-forward is canceled. No waiting for the following autumn.

Effective immediately
Provision 02

No More Clock Changes: In Either Direction

All Washington State agencies, instrumentalities, and political subdivisions are prohibited from advancing official timekeeping on any subsequent Spring Forward Date. The disruption ends permanently.

Year-round stability
Provision 03

A Built-In Federal Bridge

If Congress ever enacts a law permitting states to permanently observe Daylight Saving Time, Washington transitions to permanent PDT on the next spring date, at least 60 days after federal authorization. Either way, the clock-changing ends.

Future-proof

Why standard time, not permanent DST?

States have clear federal authority to adopt permanent standard time under 15 U.S.C. § 260a, the same authority Arizona and Hawaii use today. Permanent daylight saving time, by contrast, requires Congressional authorization that has not been enacted. The Last Fall Back Act takes the path that's available now: ending the clock change immediately, then switching to permanent daylight time automatically once Congress allows.

Read 15 U.S.C. § 260a, the Federal Uniform Time Act

The Evidence

The science is not ambiguous.

Every major medical organization that has studied the question agrees on this much: two clock changes a year cause real, measurable harm. These are transition effects, the kind that spike right after a change and settle back down within days, and they disappear completely once Washington stops switching, regardless of which time the state lands on. A separate category, level effects, is about the tradeoffs of staying on one time permanently, and it cuts in both directions. We give it a full, honest treatment on the FAQ rather than avoid it here. What follows is the well-documented part: harm caused by the switch itself.

Journal of Applied Psychology · APA
Workplace injuries jump the Monday after spring forward

Barnes & Wagner's analysis of national injury data found that on the Monday after the spring change, workers slept about 40 minutes less and sustained 5.7% more workplace injuries, with 68% more workdays lost. No comparable spike follows the fall change, when an hour is gained.

Peer-Reviewed · apa.org
American Heart Association
Cardiac events spike after spring forward

The AHA documents the evidence that the spring clock change is associated with measurable increases in myocardial infarction and stroke in the days immediately following. Sleep disruption triggers cardiovascular events in vulnerable individuals.

AHA News · heart.org
Sleep Medicine · Elsevier
Ischemic strokes rise right after the transition

Sipilä et al. examined a decade of Finnish stroke hospitalizations and found ischemic stroke incidence elevated in the first two days after a daylight saving transition, with older adults and cancer patients the most susceptible.

Peer-Reviewed · Sleep Medicine
Current Biology · Cell Press
Fatal car crashes spike after "spring forward"

Fritz et al. analyzed 22 years of U.S. crash data and found fatal motor-vehicle accidents rose about 6% in the workweek after the spring transition, roughly 28 additional deaths a year, with the risk highest in the morning and on the western edge of each time zone.

Peer-Reviewed · cell.com
Dtsch Ärztebl Int · Meta-Analysis
Heart-attack risk rises after the spring transition

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis pooling 12 studies across 10 countries found a measurable increase in acute myocardial infarction risk in the days after spring forward, with no matching rise after the fall change back.

Systematic Review · PMC
University of Washington
Deer-vehicle collisions spike after "fall back," too

Cunningham, Prugh and colleagues analyzed more than a million deer-vehicle collisions across 23 states and found collisions rose about 16% in the week after the fall shift to standard time, as evening commutes move abruptly into darkness during deer breeding season. Unlike the cardiac and workplace-injury literature, this is a harm the fall change causes on its own.

Peer-Reviewed · washington.edu
Washington State Legislature
Washington already voted to end the clock change in 2019

Substitute House Bill 1196, sponsored by then-Rep. Marcus Riccelli and signed into law in 2019 as Chapter 297, expresses the legislature's intent to observe permanent daylight saving time, but only if Congress acts. That authorization hasn't come. The Last Fall Back Act takes the path that doesn't require waiting.

Public Law · app.leg.wa.gov

2027 Legislative Session · Jan–Apr
The Fastest Path

The 2027 session runs from mid-January through late April, with bills typically signed into law by early May. If the Legislature passes the Last Fall Back Act, the job is done. No ballot initiative needed.

March 2027
File Initiative to the Legislature

If the session stalls, we file an Initiative to the Legislature with the Washington Secretary of State. The filing window opens while the 2027 session is still underway.

Mar – Dec 2027
Signature Collection

Gathering signatures toward a target of about 386,000, through volunteers and partner networks. That is a 20 to 25 percent cushion above the 308,911 legal minimum, since some signatures are always invalidated. Signatures are due about ten days before the 2028 legislative session opens. (Washington Secretary of State.)

2028 Legislative Session, Then November 2028
The 2028 Legislature, Then the Voters

A certified initiative goes to the 2028 Legislature first. They can adopt it outright, which makes it law without a ballot vote, or decline to act, in which case it appears on the November 2028 ballot and a majority vote makes it law.

November 2028: Immediately After Enactment
The Last Clock Change

Washington falls back in November 2028. The spring-forward scheduled for March 2029 is canceled. Washington is on permanent Pacific Standard Time: forever, or until Congress authorizes states to adopt permanent DST, at which point a smooth, planned transition follows.
